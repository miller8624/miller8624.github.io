Three full-body YMCA sessions cover every major movement with a different emphasis. Move days to fit family life; keep a recovery day between lifts.

Your YMCA templates

Create A, B, and C as routines in Strong. Returning to consistent lifting: use 2 working sets in week one, then 3 if recovery is good. Warm up 5–8 minutes and do light practice sets. Stop with 2–3 good reps left; rest 90–150 seconds (up to 3 minutes on demanding sets).

Progression: Once every working set reaches the top of its rep range with clean form twice, use the smallest available weight increase and return to the lower end. No max attempts needed. Machines are a useful solo option; ask YMCA staff for setup help.

Cardio that builds with you

Steps: Your goal stays at least 10,000/day. Add short walks to your current routine, then build toward the goal over 1–2 weeks if needed. Spread movement across the day.

Weeks 1–2: Two 20-minute easy walks, bikes, or Fitness+ low-impact sessions. Comfortable conversation pace.

Weeks 3–4: Two 25–30-minute sessions if recovery is good.

Weeks 5+: Gradually work toward 150 minutes of moderate activity per week; brisk step walks count. Add 5 minutes per session when comfortable.

Optional running: After 30 minutes of pain-free brisk walking feels easy, try 1 minute easy jog / 2 minutes walk × 6, twice weekly on nonconsecutive days. Repeat a week before increasing. Walking is enough if running is uncomfortable.

Home fallback: 2 rounds of 8–12 chair squats, 6–12 wall/incline pushups, 10–15 glute bridges, 6–10 bird dogs per side, and a 20-second plank. Rest as needed; substitute for a lift when the Y is unavailable.

Choose Fitness+ cycling, treadmill walking, yoga, or core. Avoid adding hard HIIT while adapting to lifting. Sharp pain, dizziness, or chest symptoms mean stop; seek appropriate care. Adapt around known restrictions.

A starting trial, then evidence

Your original 200 g carbs × 4 + 75 g fat × 9 + 180 g protein × 4 = 2,195 kcal. That is a coherent macro budget, but weight alone cannot tell us whether it produces the right deficit.

Your calorie needs depend on your individual profile and weight trend. Starting nearer 2,350–2,500 kcal is also reasonable if hunger or recovery makes 2,195 hard; adjust carbs/fat in Settings while keeping protein near 180 g. Age, height, activity, health, and weight trend matter. Use this as a provisional 2–3 week trial if appropriate for you. Aim initially for about 0.5–1% of body weight per week at most; a gentler 0.5–1.5 lb/week is a practical starting pace for protecting recovery. CDC describes gradual loss of about 1–2 lb/week.

Compare weekly average weights after the first two weeks. If loss is faster than ~2 lb/week, or hunger, fatigue, and strength worsen, increase food and reassess. If averages stay flat for 2–3 weeks with consistent logging, consider a small 100–150 kcal adjustment or a little more walking. Change one thing at a time; do not keep cutting automatically.

Discuss high-protein dieting with your clinician if you have kidney disease or another relevant condition. Avoid automatically eating back exercise calories.

Keep dinner flexible

Reserve space: Budget roughly 700–800 calories for the shared evening meal, then adjust breakfast, lunch, and snacks to the actual recipe.

Anchor protein: Aim for roughly 40–50 g at three meals, then fill the remaining protein with a snack. Greek yogurt, eggs, chicken, tofu, fish, beans, or cottage cheese can help.

Build the plate: Start with a protein serving and vegetables, add the family's starch, and portion rich sauces, oils, and cheese. No separate dinner required.

Log recipes: Divide total recipe macros by servings. Estimates are useful; consistent estimates beat abandoning the log. Include cooking oils.

Example budget: Breakfast 450 + lunch 600 + dinner 750 + snacks 395 = 2,195 kcal. Food choices and portions determine macros; these are flexible allocations.

One higher-calorie meal does not require punishment or skipping food tomorrow. Resume your usual routine.
